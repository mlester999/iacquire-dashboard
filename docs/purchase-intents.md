# Purchase intents

A purchase intent is the verified path from an invited buyer to a client workspace. It freezes the approved commercial offer, gives the buyer one public link, and records payment evidence from one of two sources: a Super Admin who checked the money, or PayMongo's **signed** notice for an online checkout the buyer opened from that link. Only then does it use the existing client-creation and billing services to make the workspace. It is not a second billing system and it does not create a client from a demo prospect.

## The flow

1. A manager with `agency.prospects.view` sees the list; a Super Admin with `agency.prospects.manage` creates an invitation. The API returns the public URL **once**; only its hash is stored. The Super Admin may allow the workspace to be created by itself after a verified online payment (this needs the billing and client-creation permissions as well, because the system will act with them).
2. The buyer opens `/purchase/<token>`, sees the frozen price and confirms. That records `started`; it creates no user, organization, payment, subscription or entitlement.
3. **Online:** when the Super Admin has switched online checkout on and PayMongo is ready, the confirmed buyer sees "Pay US$1,499 securely online". Pressing it opens one hosted checkout and sends the buyer to PayMongo. Nothing is charged by this site, and this site never sees a card. PayMongo charges Philippine pesos, so the dollar fee is converted at the agency's published rate; the rate, its source and its date are stored with the checkout. Backing out and pressing again reopens the same checkout, so a second tab or press never charges twice.
4. PayMongo tells the application (a signed webhook) that the checkout was paid. The checkout is found by the id we stored, then the reference, mode, currency and amount must equal what we stored. Only then is the intent `payment_verified` with `payment_source = provider`. The page the buyer returns to (`/purchase/return/<checkout reference>`) reads that stored status; coming back proves nothing, and it never says "paid" on its own.
5. If the inviting Super Admin allowed it, the workspace is created now, under that person's permissions **read again at that moment** (a person's decision does not outlive their access). Otherwise a Super Admin presses "Create client workspace".
6. **By hand:** a Super Admin checks the money in the bank, PayPal or PayMongo dashboard and records the evidence; recording it closes any online checkout that is open so the buyer cannot pay twice. The amount must be exactly the frozen setup fee.

The statuses are `invited`, `started`, `payment_verified`, `provisioned`, `cancelled` and `expired`. An open link past its day reads as expired even before a job writes that word. A verified payment is never cancelled quietly: with no hold it needs the refund path; with a hold it can be closed without a workspace (below), which reads "Closed after payment". The online checkouts have their own statuses: `creating`, `pending`, `paid`, `failed`, `cancelled`, `expired`, `mismatch`, `refunded`, `disputed`.

## What happens when it does not go to plan

| Situation | What the system does |
| --- | --- |
| Online checkout switched off, PayMongo not connected, no exchange rate, or live keys without the payment review | The buyer is not offered a Pay button and is told iAcquire will verify the payment another way; the Super Admin sees the reason in words |
| The buyer backs out of PayMongo's page | The return page says they left, nothing was charged and how to come back (it never claims the checkout is cancelled: only PayMongo's notice can say that). The purchase link still offers Pay and reopens the same checkout |
| The same signed notice arrives twice | Acknowledged and ignored; the event ledger makes retries and replays harmless |
| The paid amount or currency differs from what we stored | The checkout becomes `mismatch`, the purchase is put on hold, the team gets a critical notification, nothing is created |
| The mode (test or live) differs | Rejected |
| A refund or dispute is reported **before** the workspace exists | The checkout becomes `refunded` or `disputed`, the purchase is put on hold, the team is told, nothing is created or removed; a Super Admin then releases the hold or closes the purchase (below) |
| A refund or dispute is reported **after** the workspace exists | Reported on the workspace's own payment row (the payments screen shows "PayMongo reported a refund … nothing was changed automatically"); the workspace and the purchase do not change |
| Someone pays a checkout for a purchase that was already paid by hand, already provisioned, or cancelled | The payment is recorded on the checkout as a duplicate or late payment and the team is told to refund it in PayMongo; nothing new is created |
| Creating the workspace fails after a verified payment | The reason is written on the purchase and in its timeline, the team is told, "Create client workspace" finishes the same workspace (it never makes a second one), and the renewal sweep retries up to six times |
| The Super Admin who allowed automatic creation is no longer active, or lost the permissions | Nothing is created; the purchase waits for a person |
| A checkout nobody finished | Closed unpaid after 48 hours by the sweep; a payment that arrives for it later is still recorded, because the money is real whenever it comes |

**Resolving a hold.** A held purchase shows its reason in words and gives a Super Admin two ways out, each needing the person's own words and an audit row:

* **Release the hold** (needs the same billing and client-creation permissions as creating the workspace, because it re-enables that): "What did you check with the provider?" at least 10 characters. The hold is cleared; the checkout that paid goes back from `refunded` or `disputed` to `paid` (the person judged the report not to apply, and the workspace adopts that checkout as its evidence); a purchase that was never verified (a mismatch) lets the buyer try again; a verified online payment that the inviting Super Admin allowed to provision itself carries on at once, under that person's re-read permissions. The timeline says a person released the hold; the note is in the audit record, which only agency code reads.
* **Close without a workspace** (only while a hold exists on a verified payment): "Why is it being closed?" at least 10 characters. No workspace is created, the payment record stays on the purchase (migration 0103 relaxes 0101's rule to "verified or provisioned needs evidence"), any open checkout is closed, the status reads "Closed after payment", and the buyer's address may be invited again. A verified payment with **no** hold is still never cancelled quietly: use the refund path.

## Inviting the buyer: the email, its delivery record, and sending again

*Added by migration 0119. Everything here runs on the server; the browser never sends email and never holds a provider key.*

**Creating an invitation.** Super Admin, Purchase intents, "Invite a buyer". The box "Email the invitation to the buyer now" is **ticked by default** and says what it will do ("will send them a branded email with their secure purchase link"); pressing the button first shows a confirmation naming the address, so nothing is sent before the Super Admin has confirmed. The server then, in one transaction, freezes the approved price (US$1,499 and the optional US$299/month, unchanged), creates the purchase, writes a **delivery record** (`purchase_intent_deliveries`) and, when email was asked for and is set up, sends the email and records what the email provider answered.

**The email** is branded and mobile-friendly HTML with a plain-text part: the buyer's first name, the company, the setup price, the optional maintenance plan (selected or not), the reference, the link's expiry **in the buyer's own time zone**, a "Review your purchase" button and the same link written out underneath. It says nothing is charged until the buyer chooses to pay on the payment provider's own page. It is sent from `EMAIL_FROM`; a placeholder sender or a missing key means email is *not ready* and the form says so, in words, before anything is pressed.

**What the delivery record can say.** The words are fixed (`src/lib/purchase-intents/delivery.ts`) and are the same on the creation answer, the Invitations list and the detail page:

| Status | Meaning | Set by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pending | The email is being sent. After 5 minutes with no result the page says no result was recorded and offers to send again | the create/resend request |
| Sent to the email provider | The provider **accepted** the message. This is *not* "delivered": the page says it left iAcquire, not that it reached the inbox | the provider's answer to the send |
| Delivered | The provider reported delivery to the buyer's mail server (a spam complaint also counts: it reached an inbox). Not proof the buyer read it | **only** the provider's signed webhook (`email.delivered`, `email.complained`) |
| Bounced | The buyer's mail server did not accept it; shown with the kind (permanent / transient). Final: a late "delivered" notice cannot undo it | the signed webhook (`email.bounced`) |
| Failed | The provider refused or could not be reached, or took the message and then did not send it (`email.failed`, `email.suppressed`) | the send, or the signed webhook |
| Manual sharing required | No email was sent (email switched off, no key, placeholder sender, or the Super Admin unticked it). The invitation still exists and the link is shown **once** to share by hand, with the reason | the create/resend request |

The provider's notices reach `/api/webhooks/resend` (`RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET`; Svix signature and replay checks). The message is matched by the provider's own id, which is stored when it accepts the message; a notice that matches nothing is ignored, and a notice can never move a hand-shared link. **Without that webhook the status stays "Sent to the email provider" for good**: that is the honest answer, not a bug. A notice that arrives in the few milliseconds before the send is recorded finds nothing to match and is ignored, so that invitation also stays "Sent".

**Check email setup.** Under the email box a Super Admin can press "Check email setup". The server asks the email provider (read-only, with iAcquire's own key) whether the key is accepted, the sender's domain is in the account and verified for sending, and the account's webhook to this site is enabled, subscribed to `email.delivered`, `email.bounced` and `email.complained`, and signed with `RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET`. Also subscribe it to `email.failed` and `email.suppressed` to see a message the provider took and then did not send. The check sends nothing, shows each finding as passed or not passed, and says it could not be checked when the key can only send (a sending-only key cannot read a domain's status). When email is off or the sender is a placeholder it names the setting and makes no call. Super Admin only, 12 a person per hour, one audit row (`email_setup.checked`); the key is never returned (`tests/purchase-email-setup.test.ts`).

**The link is never stored.** Only `sha256(token)` is stored on the purchase, as before. The email's own text carries the link; the outbox keeps a copy with the link cut out (`[secure link not stored]`), and no delivery row, timeline event, audit row, rate-limit row or log line holds it (`tests/purchase-invitation-delivery.test.ts` reads every one of those tables for the token). The creation answer returns the link once; a repeated request with the same key returns the same invitation and **no link**, because it cannot be shown again.

**Pressing twice is safe.** The form carries an idempotency key. The same key and the same buyer answer the invitation already made (one purchase, one link, one email); two requests arriving together meet at the database's own rules (a unique key per agency and one open purchase per buyer address) and the loser reads the winner's invitation; a key reused for a different buyer is refused.

**Sending again.** "Send again with a new link" (and "New link for hand sharing") is Super Admin only, allowed while the purchase is invited, started or expired, and refused once the payment is verified, the workspace exists or the purchase is cancelled. It takes a row lock, makes a **new** token (the old link stops working at once, which is also the answer to a lost or leaked link), writes a delivery record, a timeline entry and an audit row, and emails it. Limits: one new link per minute and six a day per purchase, and 30 invitations or new links per Super Admin per hour. A repeated press with the same key finds the link already made instead of making another, so a double click cannot kill the link that was just emailed.

**The list is live.** The Invitations list on the page is the server's list merged with what this page has just made or changed, so a new invitation appears and the counter updates **without a refresh**; the next server read reconciles it. The creation answer, the list and the detail page read the same delivery record (reference, recipient, expiry in the Super Admin's zone, status, actions).

**From the demo.** The demo's "Request my purchase link" only *asks*: it records a prospect event and notifies the team; it creates no invitation, no workspace and no payment. A Super Admin reviews the request and invites from the prospect's page (the invitation is linked to the prospect by the `created` event's `prospectId`, no schema change), and the prospect's page lists its invitations.

## Payment provider setup and "Check connection"

*Added by migration 0119.* Billing has a **Payment providers** tab (Super Admin) that is the one place the state of PayMongo and PayPal is told, and the Purchase intents page, Billing's Rates tab and the buyer's Pay button all read the same server-derived health (`src/server/billing/provider-health.ts`), so they cannot disagree.

* **Six states**, in words: Not connected, Configuration incomplete, Ready for testing, Test verified, Live approval required, Ready for live payments (`src/lib/payment-providers/readiness.ts`). "Test verified" and "Ready for live payments" need a passing check made with the **same configuration**: a hash of the non-secret shape of it (which settings exist, the mode, the payment methods, a hash of each credential) is stored with each check, so changing a key, the mode or the webhook setting makes the old check stop counting.
* **Check connection** makes real, authorized, read-only calls and records the outcome (`payment_provider_checks`, append-only, no secret in it): PayMongo `GET /v1/payments?limit=1` (proves the secret key) and, best-effort, `GET /v1/webhooks` (is an enabled webhook registered for this site's address with `checkout_session.payment.paid`?); PayPal an OAuth token and the webhook list through the existing adapter. It never creates a charge. Super Admin only, 12 checks per person per hour. If the provider cannot be reached it says so; it never reports a connection it did not prove.
* **No raw secret ever goes through the browser.** The page has no input for a key. It shows each setting by its exact variable name as *Set* or *Missing* (`PAYMONGO_SECRET_KEY`, `PAYMONGO_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `PAYMONGO_PAYMENT_METHODS` (optional), the USD to PHP exchange rate in Billing; `PAYPAL_CLIENT_ID`, `PAYPAL_CLIENT_SECRET`, `PAYPAL_WEBHOOK_ID`, `PAYPAL_ENVIRONMENT`), never its value, with the Vercel steps to add them and the webhook address and events to register in the provider's dashboard.
* **The switch cannot suggest payments work when they cannot.** "Online checkout for purchases" cannot be turned on while no provider is ready; if it was on and the last provider stops being ready it reads "On, but blocked", with the reason. A live key additionally needs the agency's payment and tax review **and** a passing check for the current configuration. **Real-money payments stay off until the founder approves them**; nothing in this build switches them on.

## Separation from prospects and clients

* The purchase intent lives in its own tables and belongs to the agency workspace (`agency_org_id`), never to a client workspace. Its online checkouts (`purchase_intent_checkouts`) cannot live in `payments`, which belongs to a client workspace that does not exist yet; when the workspace is created the checkout's evidence is **adopted** into that workspace's own payment row (`src/server/billing/provider-setup-payment.ts`), so a later refund or dispute notice for it finds the canonical row.
* It is not a demo prospect: the demo funnel has its own tables, caps and retention. Converting a demo prospect remains a separate Super Admin action.
* A client organization is written only by `createClientTenant`, with the buyer's normal invitation and membership rules.
* The public page exposes one frozen offer for the token holder; it exposes no list, no other buyer and no internal note.

## Security

* Token: 32 random bytes, base64url; only `sha256(token)` is stored. The URL is marked `no-store`, `no-referrer` and `noindex` at the proxy, and the page is rate-limited per address. The token is never given to PayMongo: the buyer comes back by the checkout's own reference.
* The public actions accept only a token-shaped value; they cannot choose an amount, a currency, a company or a workspace. Starting a checkout is limited to 12 per address per hour and 8 per purchase per hour, and a purchase may open at most 8 checkouts in its life.
* The amount is the database's: a trigger copies the frozen fee onto the checkout and refuses any other figure, and a checkout's terms never change after creation.
* Reads: RLS on the intent, checkout and timeline tables, select-only for `iaq_app_user`, scoped to the one agency workspace and `agency.prospects.view`. No browser role has a grant.
* Writes: trusted server code only. Every admin action re-reads the signed-in person's active role and permissions; the service refuses managers for creating intents or recording payment, and requires the client-creation and billing permissions to provision.
* Audit: each transition writes a `purchase_intent_events` row and an `audit_logs` row. The timeline holds ids, fixed words, amounts and methods, never the raw token or the buyer's free-text note. The timeline table is append-only.
* The simulated PayMongo (`PAYMONGO_API_BASE`) is refused in production, for any address that is not this machine's, and with any key that is not a test key (`src/server/env.ts`), and the isolated QA environment refuses every other PayMongo setting.
* The simulated PayPal (`PAYPAL_API_BASE`, `scripts/paypal-sim.mjs`) is refused in production, for any address that is not this machine's, for the live environment, and for a client id that is not the simulator's made-up one (`src/server/env.ts`); the isolated QA environment refuses every other PayPal setting (`tests/paypal-simulator-guard.test.ts`). It changes where the server's PayPal API calls go and nothing else: the approval links stay ordinary `https://www.sandbox.paypal.com` addresses that the application checks as it would in production, and the browser run answers those from the simulator itself (`scripts/wave3-paypal-qa.mjs`), passing nothing to the network.

## Migrations and rollback

`0101_purchase_intents.sql` adds `purchase_intents` and `purchase_intent_events`, the agency guard and event guards, the read policy and the `app.purchase_intents_allowed()` / `app.purchase_intent_agency_org()` helpers. `0103_purchase_checkouts.sql` adds `purchase_intent_checkouts` and its guard, the evidence-source, hold and provisioning-attempt columns on the intent, the new timeline kinds, the maintenance-period columns on `payments`, and the agency's checkout switch (off by default). Both have rollbacks in `supabase/rollbacks/` that refuse while any purchase, checkout, online-paid intent or maintenance-period payment exists. Both were rehearsed on embedded Postgres with the whole chain; **neither has been applied to a hosted database**.

`0119_purchase_invitation_delivery.sql` is additive: `purchase_intent_deliveries` and `payment_provider_checks` (both written only by trusted server code, select-only to the application role and scoped to the one agency workspace, with the guard trigger and the append-only trigger), and one more timeline kind, `invitation_email`. Its rollback (`supabase/rollbacks/0119_purchase_invitation_delivery.down.sql`) **refuses while any delivery, provider check or invitation-email timeline entry exists**, because dropping them would erase what was really sent; it must run before the 0101 rollback. The code reads both tables defensively (`to_regclass`), so a deploy that reaches production before the migration shows no delivery information instead of failing, but the release order is still **migration first, then code** (`docs/purchase-invitation-and-demo-release-2026-11-03.md`).

## Not claimed

PayMongo itself was never called: the signed notices that verify a payment in the browser run came from a simulator written from PayMongo's documentation, and the real payloads have never been seen from a PayMongo account. No real purchase-invitation email was sent (every email test uses a stand-in provider), no real provider connection check was made, Resend's real delivery and bounce notices were never seen, no card-on-file recurring charge exists, PayPal is only an adapter id (nothing creates a PayPal checkout), and no hosted migration or production workspace exists. The US$299 plan is a recorded buyer choice at purchase time and a plan the client can pay for month by month afterwards; it is not an automatic subscription.
