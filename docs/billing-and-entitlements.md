# iAcquire's own billing: setup payment clearance, the manual payment ledger and entitlements

This is iAcquire's billing: the one-time setup fee and the optional monthly plan that iAcquire charges its clients. It is **not** the invoices a client sends its own customers ([invoices-and-payments.md](invoices-and-payments.md)); the two share no table, no service and no money code. Provider credit top-ups and the metering ledger are in [finance-and-billing.md](finance-and-billing.md).

Status: **Phase 7, milestones M1 (setup payment clearance and the manual ledger) and M2 (one entitlement helper and lifecycle hardening) built and verified locally** (embedded Postgres, replaced HTTP layer, hand-written PayMongo fixtures). Nothing has been applied to a hosted database, enabled, or tried against PayMongo. The scheduler has never run in production, so the renewal job below has never run anywhere but in tests.

Code: `src/server/billing/{clearance,after-clearing,manual,payments,payments-view}.ts`, `src/server/billing/providers/{types,paymongo}.ts`, `src/server/maintenance/{payment,lapse,renewal}.ts`, `src/server/entitlements.ts`, `src/lib/{billing-clearance,billing-renewal,entitlements}.ts`, `src/app/(admin)/admin/billing/actions.ts`, `src/components/admin/payments-workspace.tsx`, migration `0099_payment_controls.sql` (rollback in `supabase/rollbacks`). Tests: `tests/billing-clearance.test.ts`, `billing-manual.test.ts`, `payment-gate.test.ts`, `billing-providers.test.ts`, `billing-ui.test.tsx`, `billing-reported-events.test.ts`, `billing-renewal.test.ts`, `billing-lifecycle.test.ts`, `billing-lifecycle-ui.test.tsx`, `billing-plan-controls.test.ts`, `entitlements-rules.test.ts`, `entitlements-matrix.test.ts`, `tests/support/billing.ts`, `tests/support/billing-plan.ts`.

## The rule

Payment is evidence, not a click. The setup fee is **cleared** only by a payment a provider confirmed (PayMongo's signed notice) or that a Super Admin saw arrive and recorded with a reference, or by a **waiver** with a stated reason. One SQL function, `app.setup_payment_cleared(org)`, answers the question for every gate: paid or waived.

| Where the gate sits | What it stops until the fee is cleared |
| --- | --- |
| `requestProvisioning` | No AI employee profile is requested (the step that costs iAcquire money). The questionnaire itself is not gated. |
| `activateSetup` | A `payment_unverified` blocker, listed first, on the activation review. Marking the setup ready for review is not blocked: payment is not a setup task. |
| `maintenanceEligibility`, `applyVerifiedPayment` (activate) | The optional plan cannot be requested or started. A plan already running is untouched. |
| `deriveEmployeeReadiness` | A card that is not yet working reads "Setup required: Your AI team starts after your setup payment is confirmed". |

**Never gated by payment or by a lapse:** core CRM, the inbox, the six employees' chat, the website, booking, social text posts, and anything already running. `tests/payment-gate.test.ts` proves it two ways: a scan that lists every file allowed to ask the clearance question (a new caller fails the test), and a representative operation in CRM, booking and social with the fee refunded and the plan expired.

A **full refund** of the payment that cleared the fee sets the status to `refunded`: nothing new is provisioned and a new plan cannot start, but the activated setup, the provisioning requests and the running employees are left exactly as they are. A fresh payment or a waiver clears it again.

## What a Super Admin can do (`billing.agency_manage`, Super Admin only)

* **Record a payment received outside a provider** (`recordManualPayment`): kind (setup fee, maintenance, custom project, domain, other), exact US-dollar amount, how it arrived, the bank reference or receipt number, the day it arrived, and what they checked. The payment row, the status it changes and the AI employees' provisioning requests commit together. The same reference never makes two payments (a repeat returns the first record; a different workspace, kind or amount is refused). A setup-fee payment must equal the agreed fee; anything else is a waiver. A maintenance payment names the period it covers and goes through the same transition (`applyVerifiedPayment`) as an agency member's verification, so the plan and the money record agree; if the plan's rules refuse, the payment is rolled back with the refusal.
* **Waive the setup fee** (`waiveSetupFee`) with a reason of 10 to 500 characters. Saved with who and when; **the client is told the fee was waived, not why**. An open setup checkout is cancelled so nobody can pay after the waiver.
* **Record a refund** (`recordRefundAction`) that was already made with the provider or by hand. It records; it does not send money.

The billing form on the client's page no longer types a status in: `updateBilling` may only toggle "not invoiced" and "invoiced" (neither clears anything), and cannot mark activation "active" while the fee is not cleared. The database enforces the same: "paid" needs a verified setup payment of the same workspace, "waived" needs a reason, a manual payment needs its evidence and cannot have it edited afterwards, one setup checkout can be open at a time.

Screens: the **Payments** tab on `/admin/billing` (every payment, who proved it, clients still waiting, record and refund controls) and the **Billing** tab on a client (where the fee stands, waiver details, that client's payments, the same controls, then the invoice and terms form). Everything is labelled for what it is: "Recorded by hand: Bank transfer" is a person's record; "PayMongo, confirmed by its signed notice" is a provider's. No text says a payment was detected unless PayMongo's signature was verified.

## Provider adapters

`PaymentProviderAdapter` (`providers/types.ts`): `notReady()`, `expectedLivemode()`, `verify(raw body, headers)` and `parse(raw body)` to one `NormalizedPaymentEvent`. The shared handler (`handleProviderWebhook` in `payments.ts`) never reads a provider's payload: the organisation always comes from OUR stored payment row; the reference, amount, currency and mode must all agree; the event-id ledger (now keyed by provider and event id) makes retries harmless. PayMongo is the first adapter and wraps the functions in `paymongo.ts` unchanged, so `tests/billing.test.ts` passes untouched. **PayPal does not exist**: `tests/billing-providers.test.ts` drives the handler with a fake second adapter only to prove the rules do not depend on PayMongo's payload. Settling PayPal's event names and verification belongs to milestone M6 and needs an account.

A setup-fee payment confirmed after the fee was already paid or waived is recorded (the money arrived), changes neither the status nor the requests, and raises a reconciliation alert so a person decides whether to refund it.

## Existing clients (founder decision 1, open)

Recommended default, built as one named statement in `0099_payment_controls.sql` ("DATA STEP 2") and lifted out of the file by a test: every workspace whose setup is **already activated** and whose fee is not paid, waived or refunded becomes `waived` with the recorded reason **"Activated before the payment gate"** (`ACTIVATED_BEFORE_GATE_REASON`). A workspace still in setup is not touched and needs a payment or a waiver. A fee marked "paid" by hand before payments were recorded stays "paid" and is shown as having no payment record. The rollback leaves the backfilled status alone (it is valid before 0099 too).

## Notifications and privacy

New kinds: `billing_setup_payment_required` (agency and owner, once per workspace, when onboarding is done and only the payment is missing), `billing_setup_cleared`, `billing_refund_recorded`, and `billing_renewal_due` (the renewal reminders and the "paid period ended" notice below). The waiver reason, who waived, the bank reference, the checking note and the verifier are readable only by agency code: the application role has no grant on those columns, the client export omits them, and the activity timeline shows an action name, not its metadata. Audit entries record who and what, and keep the bank reference on the payment row only.

## Entitlements: one answer for the plan

`workspaceEntitlements(db, orgId, now)` (`src/server/entitlements.ts`, with the pure rules in `src/lib/entitlements.ts`) answers four things from the database once: whether Automation Studio is available (`studio`, the same object every gate and the engine used before), whether **managed media storage** is available (`managedStorage`, exactly the same answer), where the plan stands (`maintenance`: its status, the day it is paid through, whether that day has passed, days left) and whether the setup fee is cleared (`setupCleared`). The fee is a separate question and never changes what the plan entitles.

Who asks: every Automation Studio gate and the workflow engine (through `studioEntitlement`), managed media storage for social posts (`getMediaPolicy`), the portal menu (the shell reads the four plan facts its role may see through `app.automation_studio_plan` and applies the same rule, `studioEntitlementOf`), and the Setup Center's storage line. Before this stage the media policy ran its own query (`active`, `past_due`, `cancel_requested`), so a plan whose grace period had ended kept its storage until a scheduler that has never run in production marked it expired. Now it ends at the same instant as Automation Studio, from the clock. `tests/entitlements-matrix.test.ts` proves the four consumers agree for every plan state and fails if another file decides an entitlement from the plan table.

What is **never** an entitlement: core CRM, the inbox, the six AI employees, the website, booking and social text posts. No plan, lapse, refund or unpaid fee touches them. The matrix test proves it with a representative operation in each of those modules for a workspace that never had a plan, one with a declined request, a cancelled plan, an expired plan with a refunded fee, and a past-due plan whose grace period ended, after the scheduler's own lapse pass has run. It also shows that every `assertStudioEntitled` and `studioEntitlement` caller in the workflow engine is on a premium path (a version that uses Automation Studio steps), by scanning the source, with a negative control so the scan can fail.

A paid period that has **ended** is information, not a lapse: an active plan stays entitled after its paid-through day until a person records the payment, a person records a failed payment (which starts the disclosed grace period), or the automatic switch below does. The agency's plan card says so ("The paid period ended 3 days ago and no payment has been recorded since. The plan is still active.") using the agency's own calendar day.

## Renewals and lapse (milestone M2)

The scheduled job `maintenanceRenewalSweep` (every 360 minutes, `src/server/maintenance/renewal.ts`) is dormant like every job: it runs only when the scheduler does and, in a staged start, only when named in `SCHEDULER_JOBS_ONLY`. It works in pages of 100 plans inside a 30-second budget (the tick has 120 seconds), one plan at a time under a row lock, so two runs together do each plan once. For an **active** plan that has a paid-through day (judged in the agency's time zone):

* **7 days and 1 day before** that day the team and the client's owners are reminded, once each per plan, level and paid period (the notice key carries the day, so a renewed period gets fresh reminders). The wording never says a payment was seen: "Check whether the next payment has arrived and record it so the plan carries on. The portal does not detect payments by itself."
* **After** that day, with no payment recorded, the team is told once. Nothing else changes while the automatic switch is off.
* **Only if a Super Admin switched "mark past due automatically" on** (`agency_billing_settings.auto_past_due`, **off** by default, a switch on `/admin/billing` with who and when stored) a plan whose paid period ended **two or more days ago** is marked past due as the system, with the same grace period a person's record starts (`STUDIO_PAYMENT_GRACE_DAYS`, default 7 days). The owner is told "iAcquire hasn't recorded a payment" (not "a payment failed": nothing is known about a payment that was never seen) and the exact date. The job itself never expires, pauses, refunds or revokes anything; a plan it marks follows the usual path: a recorded payment brings it back, and with none by the end of the grace period the existing plan sweep expires it and pauses its Automation Studio workflows (nothing is deleted).
* **Checkouts nobody completed** (pending for 48 hours) are closed (`expired`, audited), so an abandoned one cannot block a second setup checkout for ever. A payment that arrives for a closed checkout later is still recorded.

It skips suspended and archived workspaces, calls no payment provider and sends nothing outside the portal itself (the notices it creates are emailed by the notification job like any other warning). A plan row whose pass fails is skipped and tried again next run, with nothing half done.

Other lifecycle fixes in this stage: a **confirmed cancellation** (and a client ending an unpaid plan) now pauses the Automation Studio workflows and sends the owner the lapse notice, exactly as an expiry does, instead of leaving that to the sweep; **reactivating a suspended workspace** restores the activation status its setup implies (active for a setup that was activated, whatever happened to the fee since; pending for one that never was), where it used to stay "Suspended"; `maintenance_subscriptions.provider` and `provider_subscription_id` are reserved for recurring billing (null today, set together, unique per provider, never readable by a signed-in role, nothing reads them).

## Refund and dispute notices from PayMongo

A signed refund or dispute notice (`payment.refund.updated`, `payment.refunded`, any event named dispute or chargeback) is **recorded, not applied**. It is found by the provider payment id the earlier paid notice gave us, written to the event ledger against our payment, audited (`billing.provider_refund_reported`, `billing.provider_dispute_reported`) and shown to the agency (a warning for a refund, critical for a dispute), with the words "Nothing was changed". The payment keeps its status and amounts, the setup fee keeps its status, and no plan, workflow, employee or workspace is touched: a notice can never take anything away. A notice for a payment we have no record of is ignored, shown on the unmatched list on `/admin/billing` and raised to the security managers. A person records the refund (so a refund is never counted twice) or answers the dispute at PayMongo. Why not apply it: the shape of these notices has never been seen from a PayMongo account, and a refund a Super Admin already recorded would be counted twice.

## Paying a plan period online (month by month)

The workspace owner (`maintenance.manage_plan`) can pay for the plan on `/maintenance`: "Pay and start the plan" for a requested plan whose core system is activated and whose setup fee is cleared, "Pay for more months" for an active one, "Pay online to restore the plan" for a past-due or expired one. The button appears only when a payment could really be taken **and confirmed** (PayMongo keys, the webhook secret and an exchange rate are set, and for a live key the payment review is confirmed): the same readiness rule as the purchase checkout, without the purchase checkout's own switch.

* The browser sends only the number of months (1, 3, 6 or 12 in the dialog, 1 to 12 in the service). The amount is the plan's own monthly price times the months, checked again against the plan row under a lock, converted to Philippine pesos at the stored rate, and stored with the checkout (`payments.kind = 'maintenance'`, `maintenance_subscription_id`, `covers_months`).
* One open payment per plan (a unique index). Backing out of PayMongo's page and pressing again for the **same** months reopens the same checkout: no second provider session, no second row, no second charge. A different number of months cannot reuse it and is told how to carry on or that the open one closes by itself within two days.
* PayMongo's signed notice, matched on reference, mode, currency and amount, is the only thing that moves the plan. It goes through the same transition (`applyVerifiedPayment`) as an agency member's recorded payment: a plan that is active and not yet past its paid-through day continues from the **day after** it (paying early loses nothing); a plan that is requested, past due, expired or already past its day starts the new period **today**. The period is whole calendar months, ending the day before the same day-of-month (clamped for short months). A past-due or expired plan returns to active and its Automation Studio workflows resume as for any recorded payment.
* A replayed notice extends nothing twice. A payment for a plan that cannot take one (cancelled) is recorded and a person is told. A refund or dispute notice is reported on the payment and changes nothing (the rule above).
* The page the owner returns to (`/maintenance?payment=…&return=success|cancelled`) never decides that a payment arrived: it says so, re-reads every few seconds for a couple of minutes, and shows the new paid-through day once the signed notice has been applied. Backing out says nothing was charged and the plan has not changed.
* The client sees the day the plan is paid through. The signed-in client role cannot read that column (migration 0071 deliberately keeps the lifecycle columns away from it), so trusted code reads it for a plan the client's own permission-checked read already returned.

This is a **renewal checkout per period**, not a subscription: nothing charges a card on file, nothing renews by itself, and PayPal has no checkout code at all. A provider subscription remains the open decision in item 10 below.

## Proposals the founder must confirm

1. Existing clients: waive those already activated (built) versus require payment or grandfather by date.
2. Whether a waiver reason may ever be shown to the client (today: never).
3. Whether a full refund after provisioning should pause the client's AI team (today: it does not).
4. The sanity limits on a recorded payment: at most US$100,000.00, dated no more than a day ahead or two years back. A typo guard, not a policy.
5. Setup-fee price, tax and refund treatment, terms and refund wording: counsel and the founder.
6. Whether the system may mark a plan past due when its paid period ended with no payment recorded (built, **off**), and after how many days (proposal: 2, so a payment recorded the next morning is never marked missed), or only notify the team.
7. The grace period (proposal: 7 days, already the default and settable per case between 1 and 30; no new setting was added) and what "past due" means for the setup fee, which has no due date today.
8. The reminder days before a paid period ends (proposal: 7 and 1) and how long a checkout may stay open (proposal: 48 hours). Each is one constant in `src/lib/billing-renewal.ts`.
9. Whether a refund or dispute notice from PayMongo should ever be applied automatically (today: never, a person records it) and whether a disputed payment should stop counting as received.
10. How the US$299 plan is collected: a provider subscription (PayPal Subscriptions, or PayMongo recurring if its contract is checked), a monthly checkout link, or by hand. Built: by hand, and a checkout per period of 1 to 12 months that the owner starts (above). Not built: a charge the provider makes by itself. The two reserved columns assume a provider subscription; nothing uses them.
