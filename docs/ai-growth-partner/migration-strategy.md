# AI Growth Partner migration strategy

**Status:** development strategy; no live client migration or runtime revocation is authorized by this document.

## Compatibility boundary

- New client workspaces receive one `growth_partner` runtime and one linked `ai_growth_partners` identity. Their setup generator does not create the six legacy runtime roles.
- Existing six-role tenants remain unchanged by the foundation migrations. Their Hermes profiles, credentials, approved configuration versions, employee settings, job history, conversation history, work schedules, audit rows, and commercial entitlements remain attached to their current identities.
- Existing setup task rows are snapshots and are not rewritten by the new default template copy. New setup instances for legacy workspaces retain legacy task wording; new partner workspaces receive Growth Partner wording.
- Legacy onboarding submission does not create a Growth Partner identity as a side effect. A tenant moves to the new model only through a separate, reviewed migration operation.
- Existing subscriptions, setup payments, purchase records, optional maintenance terms, and usage protections are not changed by the product-model migrations.

## Staged migration for an existing tenant

1. **Inventory without changes.** Read the tenant's six runtime rows, enabled state, profile identifiers and hosts, credential bindings, health evidence, active and held configuration versions, Business Brain sources, chat/conversation records, schedules, queued/running jobs, Work Board history, permissions, integrations, and purchase entitlements. Record only identifiers and status in the migration report; never copy credentials into a new profile.
2. **Prepare a tenant-specific candidate.** Create the Growth Partner identity in a disabled or setup-required state. Draft one policy/configuration version from approved tenant knowledge and explicit owner choices. Preserve provenance and distinguish confirmed facts from suggestions and unknowns. Do not reuse another tenant's Hermes credentials, profiles, memory, or tool tokens.
3. **Review and connect.** An authorized owner reviews the proposed identity, Business Brain, permissions, capabilities, and any schedule mapping. Provision a new tenant-isolated Hermes profile through the approved provisioning process, then connect its own credentials and run actual health, tool-inventory, tenant-boundary, and permitted-action checks.
4. **Reconcile work before cutover.** Compare every old enabled schedule with the proposed schedule and work assignment. Identify in-flight jobs and their idempotency keys. Pause old schedules through the tenant's authorized controls, wait for or explicitly resolve active work, verify no equivalent job is due or already queued, then enable the new schedule only after the owner approves the cutover. Keep external outreach disabled unless separately authorized.
5. **Preserve continuity.** Keep the six historical identities, configuration versions, audit records, jobs, and conversation messages available as historical records. Do not concatenate six chats into the permanent Growth Partner conversation or expose one employee's private context to another. Add an explicit historical link or a reviewed summary with source references if the owner wants continuity.
6. **Verify and monitor.** Confirm one active Growth Partner identity and one authorized active profile for that tenant, current health evidence, expected CRM/calendar capability checks, correct job ownership, no duplicate active schedules, and no cross-tenant reads. Observe the tenant through an agreed acceptance window before closing the migration.
7. **Retire only with separate approval.** Deprovision or revoke an old Hermes profile only after the migration report and acceptance evidence are reviewed and the owner explicitly approves the exact tenant and profiles. Keep the immutable business/audit history. Never delete old profile records as a shortcut.

## Rollback

- Before cutover, an owner can decline a pending proposal or withdraw an approved preparation plan. Withdrawal retains the owner decision and the preparation receipt. It restores the prior Business Brain pointer only if the exact prepared version is still active on the unbound migration identity. A newer active version stays in place.
- After cutover, rollback means pause the Growth Partner schedule and runtime, confirm no job remains in flight, and restore the previously approved legacy schedule and enabled state using the migration report. Re-enable old profiles only after checking their recorded configuration and permissions; do not restore a revoked credential or copy credentials across profiles.
- Keep both identity histories and append rollback events to the audit trail. Do not delete the Growth Partner or rewrite old conversation history.
- Schema rollback scripts restore only the fields introduced by their paired migration and should use exact-current-value guards where a template may have been manually edited. Tenant task snapshots and client data are not migration rollback targets.

## Release and approval boundary

Migration rehearsals and synthetic-tenant acceptance are development checks. They do not authorize production deployment, production database changes, a customer cutover, paid-provider use, customer messaging, payment changes, or Hermes profile revocation. Each production action still needs a separate, explicit approval naming the exact tenant, profiles, environment, and operation.

## Remaining acceptance evidence

The current development implementation has synthetic tenant and chat tests, plus migration rehearsal coverage. It does not yet prove migration against a real tenant runtime, external CRM/calendar providers, a production scheduler, or a customer-approved cutover. Those require a separately authorized staging or production acceptance run and must be reported as real provider evidence rather than inferred from a green setup badge.

## Current migration preparation

The Super Admin client detail includes tenant-scoped record counts for retained role profiles, Hermes profile and credential references, configuration review state, approved and waiting company knowledge, retained documents, and a separate breakdown of approved/waiting notes plus released/held documents attached to previous profiles and shared workspace documents. It also counts legacy conversation/message history, schedules, open and total Work Board jobs, open approvals, connected integration status rows, member permission overrides, workspace feature overrides, and the Growth Partner identity/permanent conversation. The snapshot returns no credential values or business content. For an active client with all six retained profiles, a Super Admin with workforce-management access may request a read-only schedule reconciliation preview. It shows schedule cadence, timezone, enabled state and next-run time; current Growth Partner role-policy eligibility; same-capability Partner schedule matches; and every open Work Board item's identifier, status, idempotency key and recorded schedule link. It omits saved schedule assignments and job titles, and does not write audit data or change schedules, jobs, profiles or partner setup. Capability eligibility is only a policy-level candidate; runtime readiness, workspace permissions, tool setup, owner review and cutover approval remain unverified.

The same authorized Super Admin may separately prepare the first migration artifact: an `ai_growth_partners` identity row with `migration_required` status, no runtime binding, and no active or draft Business Brain version. This idempotent action adds an audit event and nothing else. It does not import approved knowledge, create a conversation or runtime, change legacy roles/schedules/jobs/approvals/credentials, or establish cutover readiness. Existing partner setup in any other state is refused. Runtime reachability, credential validity, Hermes memory, external provider state, paid entitlements, schedule effects, and cutover readiness still require separate evidence.

The dry-run summary combines those counts with the current candidate and schedule preview. It identifies missing profile and Business Brain checks, orphaned runtimes, waiting reviews, open work or approvals, due schedules, unsupported capability mappings, and collisions between enabled schedules. It reports only counts and status. When checks are present and clear, the next stage is explicit workspace owner approval for the exact tenant, schedule changes, activation window and rollback plan. The report is advisory and must be regenerated after workspace changes or before any approval decision.

## Staging a preparation plan for the workspace owner

1. Prepare the unbound migration identity on a disposable legacy tenant. Have the workspace save a Business Brain draft with its existing review tools and finish any retained knowledge reviews. Correct disputed details in their source screens before staging.
2. In Super Admin → Clients → AI Growth Partner, select **Preview migration plan**. The preview reads the retained profile bindings and settings, exact saved Brain version and reviewed sources, workspace access overrides and current schedule mappings. Read-only supported schedules can be proposed as disabled replacements. Provider-backed or unsupported schedules remain assigned to their previous profiles, and paused schedules stay paused. Private schedule assignments, knowledge content and credential values are omitted. An enabled schedule with an invalid rule, competing enabled replacements, unresolved reviews, open work or approvals, and due replacement occurrences stop staging. A stale preview must be regenerated.
3. Select **Stage plan for owner review**. Each workspace has one current plan. Retrying the same request returns the same record. A changed plan supersedes the previous record and requires a fresh owner decision. An active owner sees the exact plan in their Growth Partner Control Center and can approve preparation or decline. Approval rechecks the tenant snapshot and records the owner, timestamp and digest. Owners can withdraw approval while retaining the original decision evidence.
4. On the disposable tenant, refresh **Preview migration plan** after approval. Select **Apply approved preparation** as the scoped Super Admin. The service verifies the owner decision against the current tenant snapshot and requires the original approver to still be an active workspace owner. It activates the exact reviewed immutable Business Brain through the normal version path and saves one tenant-scoped preparation receipt. Concurrent retries return the same receipt while the evidence remains current.
5. Verify the Partner remains unbound with status `migration_required`. The receipt reserves a local candidate runtime ID and disabled replacement schedule IDs with the approved capability, saved recurrence, cadence, interval, timezone, business-hour windows, next occurrence and legacy schedule ID. It holds no schedule assignment payload. The canonical runtime and scheduler tables, six legacy profiles, old schedules, jobs, history, permissions and integrations stay intact. Drift stops preparation and marks the owner review stale. If preparation already ran, the owner can withdraw it before a replacement is staged.

A runnable workforce row changes default role selection even without a runtime binding. Writing the runtime pointer immediately switches legacy actor routing to the Partner. A saved canonical schedule can be enabled through existing controls. Preparation therefore reserves runtime and schedule identities in a separate table and leaves the active routing pointer empty. Before any future cutover, verify the tenant-isolated Hermes profile, its own credential reference, permission/tool inventory, permanent conversation and runtime health. Reconcile the approved Brain and assignments with current tenant data, agree the activation window and rollback, resolve in-flight jobs and rehearse restart and duplicate protection. A cutover must consume current owner approval and preserve the previous identities and jobs. Obtain separate authorization for each live action. Schema downs for 0142 and 0144 refuse while corresponding receipts exist. Keep that evidence and use a forward fix.
